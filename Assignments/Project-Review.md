## Overview

You will review a **data-driven historical project**. This may be an academic paper, a piece of long-form journalism, a website, or another substantive work that focuses on a historical topic and uses data in its analysis or presentation.

Your **700–800-word written review** should evaluate the project's argument, datasets, methodology, and design. Rather than simply describing the project, focus on what works, what could be developed further, and what may be missing.

You will also share your review with the class in a **five-minute presentation** that provides an overview of the project and your evaluation of it.

## Assignment Goals

- Develop data literacy by critically evaluating data in action.
- Learn about a wider body of data-driven historical research.
- Develop skills in writing and communicating for an online readership.

## Assignment Instructions

### Choose Your Project

Choose one project to review. The project may be an academic paper, long-form journalism, website, or other substantive work that focuses on a historical topic and uses data in its analysis or presentation.

Your project must be approved by Prof. Blevins in advance.

### Review Guidelines

Your review should follow the [guidelines for Digital History Reviews](https://www.oah.org/publications/jah/submit/digital-history-reviews/) published by the *Journal of American History*. Browse a few reviews from [recent issues](https://academic-oup-com.aurarialibrary.idm.oclc.org/jah/issue) to get a sense of their length and scope.

Begin with a brief introduction to the project, including its creators and topic. Then evaluate it according to the following criteria:

- **Argument and Interpretation:** What is the project's interpretive point of view? How effectively is it communicated?
- **Data and Evidence:** What is the underlying dataset? How effectively do the creators use that data? How does the data work alongside other forms of evidence?
- **Organization and Design:** Is the project clearly and effectively organized? Is it easy to navigate? What design decisions did the creators make, and how well do they work?
- **Communication and Audience:** Is the project's argument and evidence clearly communicated? Does it have a clear audience? How well does it address that audience's needs?

## Format

This assignment will be published as a page on your personal portfolio website for an online readership. We will discuss how to build this portfolio website separately so that you have the infrastructure needed to publish your review.

Unlike printed journal reviews, your website review can and should take advantage of non-textual media. Include screenshots or other visuals that illustrate your points.
